I judge the BCI classification as Breakthrough rather than Potentially Promethean on the supplied evidence. The key reason is not that the technology is unimportant: implantable systems have demonstrated that selected people with severe paralysis can communicate and control digital devices through decoded neural signals. That is a substantial technical departure from conventional assistive interaction.

However, the evidence does not yet show broad, durable dissolution of the relevant barrier across a population, or demonstrated transformation across two human-life tiers. I would not treat prospective employment, broader social change, or human enhancement as demonstrated effects. For the large teaching hospital, a limited research role may be justified if it can protect participants and sustain the support burden. For the smaller hospital, the safer judgment is referral and monitoring, because organizational readiness—not the underlying technology—changes.
